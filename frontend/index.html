How it works

Joe-Bot is a six-stage pipeline of specialised agents, modelled on Google's AI Co-Scientist. Ideas are generated in parallel by five different reasoning strategies, de-duplicated, evaluated against real prior art, then ranked against each other in a tournament rather than scored in isolation — because absolute scores drift, but pairwise judgements hold up.

* the stages that search the live web — evolution because its variants go back through the same review
01
Parallel generation
Five strategies run at once: literature exploration, simulated expert debate, assumption inversion, first-principles decomposition, and cross-domain analogy. Each returns two concepts, and every concept must name a mechanism — not an outcome.
02
Proximity
Two ideas describing the same physics in different words are near-duplicates; the vaguer one is dropped before the expensive stage. The survivors are clustered into mechanistic families.
03
Reflection
A cheap first pass scores everything unaided; ideas that fail it skip the search and carry low scores forward. The rest get a full prior-art review weighted on novelty (30%), plausibility (25%), patentability (20%), feasibility (15%) and problem fit (10%).
04
Elo ranking
Every idea enters level, at 1200 — nothing is seeded from its review score, so the order is earned in 12 pairwise debates rather than decided in advance. A fresh judge deciding "which of these two is better" is more reliable than any absolute score. Matches favour lookalike ideas and newcomers, since those are the comparisons that actually separate a field.
05
Evolution
The top 3 are deepened with specifics that answer their own review. Combination is only attempted across unlike approaches, never between two variants of one idea, and only where the mechanisms interact to produce an effect neither parent has alone — pairs that would merely sit side by side are discarded rather than forced. Nothing is promoted for being new: a variant is reviewed like any other idea and has to beat the invention it came from to outrank it.
06
Meta-review
A final agent reads every evaluation — not just the winners — and writes up what held across the run: recurring obstacles, directions left unexplored, and the one route most worth pursuing.

You can then run another round. It inherits this round's top inventions and overview, so the system pushes into new territory instead of re-deriving what it already found — and you can steer it in plain language before it starts.
